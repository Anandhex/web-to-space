# Spatial Web Reader — Study Information, Consent, and Wellbeing Forms

Print one copy of this document per participant. It has four parts:

1. Information sheet
2. Consent form
3. Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (SSQ) — before and after
4. Operator notes (for the researcher, not the participant)

---

## Stop rule — read this first

**If at any point you feel any of the following at a Moderate or Severe
level — nausea, dizziness, or disorientation — stop immediately and tell
the operator.** The session ends right away. There is no penalty for
stopping, and the data collected up to that point is still useful to the
research. You do not need to explain or justify stopping.

The operator is also watching for outward signs of discomfort (going quiet,
swaying, sweating, reaching for the headset) independently of anything you
report, and may pause or end the session even if you have not said anything.

## 1. Information sheet

### What is this study?

You are being asked to help evaluate a prototype web browser that displays
ordinary web pages inside a VR headset, as three-dimensional spaces instead
of flat pages. The research is looking at two things: whether the way a
page's HTML is written (e.g. proper headings and landmarks vs. generic
`div`s) changes how easily you can find things in it, and whether adding
directional links and a minimap helps you navigate between pages without
getting lost.

### What will I do?

You will wear a Meta Quest VR headset and complete a series of short tasks —
about 15 in total, split into two blocks with a break in between. Each task
is one of two kinds:

- **Find a thing** — you'll be told what to look for on a page (e.g. "find
  the paragraph about X") and click on it when you find it.
- **Get somewhere** — you'll be told which page to navigate to from a small
  multi-page site, using links, doors, or corridors depending on the
  condition.

The whole session takes roughly 90 minutes, including setup, a break between
blocks, and the questionnaires below. You will try the same content
presented in three different spatial layouts (walking through rooms, an
open wall of panels, and cards on a desk-like surface) — this is by design,
not a mistake.

### Is this voluntary?

Yes. Taking part is entirely voluntary. You may stop at any time, for any
reason, with no explanation required and no consequence — this includes
stopping partway through a task, a block, or the whole session. See the
stop rule above for the specific discomfort-related version of this.

### What data is collected?

- Timestamps of your clicks and navigation actions inside the headset
  (e.g. which item you clicked, which page you moved to, how long a task
  took).
- Any free-text comments you choose to type or the operator to note during
  the session.
- Your answers on the paper forms in this document (questionnaire ratings,
  rankings, open-ended answers).

**We do not record video or audio of you, and we do not collect your name,
contact details, or any other personally identifying information.** You are
referred to only by a participant code (e.g. "P03") in all logged data and
on this form. The link between your name and your participant code, if kept
at all, is stored separately from the study data by the researcher.

### How long is data kept, and who sees it?

This is a small, local research project. Data is stored by the researcher
on their own equipment and is not shared with any third party or uploaded
to any external service beyond what is needed to analyse it. It is retained
for as long as needed to complete the research write-up, after which it may
be deleted or anonymised further. If you would like your data deleted after
the session, contact the researcher (below) and this will be honoured.

## 3. Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (SSQ)

This is modelled on the standard Kennedy et al. Simulator Sickness
Questionnaire symptom checklist and rating scale, paraphrased here rather
than reproduced verbatim. It measures 16 common symptoms of discomfort from
virtual/simulated environments. It is **not** a pass/fail test — please
answer honestly; there is no wrong answer.

**Instructions:** For each symptom, mark how much it applies to you **right
now**, using the scale None / Slight / Moderate / Severe.

You will complete this questionnaire **twice**: once **before** you put on
the headset (baseline), and once **immediately after** the session ends
(or immediately if the session is stopped early for any reason). Use the
identical form both times.
